    Quiz 8 study guide: Financial Functions

    The core Chapter 8 ideas in one place. Read this, then try the practice set. Your course notes are still the official source.

    The five functions

    They’re five views of one equation: give Excel four values and it solves for the fifth.

    FunctionAnswersSyntax
    PVWhat is it worth at the start / today?PV(rate, nper, pmt, [fv], [type])
    FVWhat will it be worth at the end?FV(rate, nper, pmt, [pv], [type])
    PMTHow much is each payment?PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type])
    RATEWhat’s the interest rate per period?RATE(nper, pmt, pv, [fv], [type])
    NPERHow many periods will it take?NPER(rate, pmt, pv, [fv], [type])

    Match the rate and periods to the compounding

    CompoundingPeriods per yearratenper (for 5 years)
    Annually1APR5
    Semiannually2APR/210
    Quarterly4APR/420
    Monthly12APR/1260
    Convert results back. NPER returns periods, so divide by periods per year to get years: =NPER(6%/12, -600, 30000)/12. RATE returns a per-period rate, so multiply to get the annual rate: =RATE(36, -300, 9000)*12.

    Compound interest

    Compound interest earns interest on earlier interest. $1,000 at 10% compounded annually is $1,100 after year 1 and $1,210 after year 2. More frequent compounding earns a little more: $1,000 at a 12% APR for one year is $1,120.00 compounded annually but $1,126.83 compounded monthly.

    Cash-flow signs

    Excel tracks the direction money moves: money in is positive, money out is negative.

    SituationFormulaResult
    Borrow $20,000 for 5 years at 6%, monthly=PMT(6%/12, 60, 20000)−$386.66 per month
    Deposit $1,000 once at 5% for 3 years=FV(5%, 3, 0, -1000)$1,157.63
    Need $10,000 in 5 years at 4%=PV(4%, 5, 0, 10000)−$8,219.27 to invest today
    Save $200/month for 10 years at 3%=FV(3%/12, 120, -200)$27,948.28

    If the payment and loan have the same sign, NPER and RATE can’t find a solution and return #NUM!.

    Optional arguments

    ArgumentIf left outUse it for
    fv0, meaning fully paid offA balloon payment (an extra lump sum due at the end) or a savings goal
    type0, payments at the end of each period1 for payments at the beginning (rent, saving at the start of the month)

    Balloon example: $25,000 for 5 years at 7% monthly with $5,000 due at the end gives =PMT(7%/12, 60, 25000, -5000) ≈ −$425.19 per month.
